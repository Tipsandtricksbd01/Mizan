Somebody bought this entire town on eBay for almost two million dollars. Then they vanished.
It was two thousand two. The town was Bridgeville, California, deep in the redwoods, two hundred sixty miles north of San Francisco.
Eighty-two acres. Ten houses. Four cabins. And its very own zip code.
The owners had tried to sell it the normal way. Nobody bit.
So they listed it online, right between used lawnmowers and baseball cards.
Bidding started at five thousand dollars.
Then the whole world found out.
Reporters drove in. TV crews called.
People who couldn't find Bridgeville on a map were bidding like it was the last concert ticket on Earth.
After one month, almost two hundred fifty bids.
When the clock hit zero, the town had sold for one million, seven hundred seventy-seven thousand, eight hundred seventy-seven dollars.
More than double the asking price.
The owners were thrilled. The headlines were everywhere.
But there was one small problem.
You see, clicking "bid" is a lot like yelling "I'll take it!" at a yard sale.
Easy to say. Much harder to pay.
The winning buyer never showed up. Neither did his money.
A year later, the owners finally admitted it.
In two thousand four, the town sold again, for real this time.
For about a million dollars less.
The new owner dreamed of a peaceful health retreat.
Then he saw the repair bills.
Two years later, Bridgeville was right back on eBay.
The town that sold for almost two million dollars just couldn't stay sold.
And now, more than twenty years later, it's heading back on the market.
So if you've ever wanted your own zip code, here's your chance.
Just this time... bring the money.
